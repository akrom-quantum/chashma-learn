# IELTS Reading

Sixty minutes. Three passages. Forty questions. No extra time for transfer.

This section of the exam is a test of **reading speed under pressure** more than it is a test of reading ability. Most students who fail IELTS Reading can read the passages — they just can't read them fast enough to answer 40 questions in 60 minutes. Everything in this course is built around that reality.

---

## Exam Structure

| Passage | Length | Difficulty | Time target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passage 1 | ~900 words | Moderate | 17 min |
| Passage 2 | ~900 words | Harder | 20 min |
| Passage 3 | ~900 words | Hardest | 20 min |
| Transfer | — | — | 3 min buffer |

**Academic Reading** draws passages from journals, textbooks, magazines, and newspapers. Expect one descriptive, one discursive (argumentative), and one analytical. At least one passage will contain detailed logical argument.

**General Training Reading** uses workplace notices, advertisements, and extracts from books. Same 40 questions, same 60 minutes, lower text difficulty. This course covers Academic by default — GT guides are flagged where they diverge.

No dictionaries. No calculators. Pencil only. Answers must be written directly on the answer sheet **during** the 60 minutes.

## The 14 Question Types

Every IELTS Reading question is one of these. Each has its own strategy — confusing them is the single biggest source of avoidable lost marks.

1. **[[multiple-choice|Multiple Choice]]** — pick A/B/C/D
2. **[[true-false-not-given|True / False / Not Given]]** — factual statements
3. **[[yes-no-not-given|Yes / No / Not Given]]** — writer's opinions
4. **[[matching-information|Matching Information]]** — find which paragraph contains X
5. **[[matching-headings|Matching Headings]]** — assign headings to paragraphs
6. **[[matching-features|Matching Features]]** — match statements to people/things
7. **[[matching-sentence-endings|Matching Sentence Endings]]** — complete half-sentences
8. **[[sentence-completion|Sentence Completion]]** — fill gaps from the passage
9. **[[summary-completion|Summary Completion]]** — fill gaps in a summary paragraph
10. **[[note-completion|Note Completion]]** — fill gaps in notes
11. **[[table-completion|Table Completion]]** — fill gaps in a table
12. **[[flow-chart-completion|Flow-chart Completion]]** — fill gaps in a process diagram
13. **[[diagram-label-completion|Diagram Label Completion]]** — label parts of a picture
14. **[[short-answer-questions|Short-answer Questions]]** — answer in ≤3 words

Roughly half of any given test is **completion** tasks (types 8–14) and **matching** tasks (types 4–7). True/False/Not Given appears in ~80% of tests. Matching Headings appears in ~60%. Know those three cold before anything else.

## Scoring

Raw score out of 40 converts to a band score from 0 to 9.0. The conversion is not linear — the top half is punishing.

| Raw score | Band |
|---|---|
| 40–39 | 9.0 |
| 38–37 | 8.5 |
| 36–35 | 8.0 |
| 34–33 | 7.5 |
| 32–30 | 7.0 |
| 29–27 | 6.5 |
| 26–23 | 6.0 |
| 22–19 | 5.5 |
| 18–15 | 5.0 |

> [!warning] The 7.0 ceiling
> The jump from Band 6.5 to Band 7.0 requires **three extra correct answers** (27 → 30). The jump from 7.0 to 7.5 requires **another three** (30 → 33). Most universities require 7.0 minimum, 6.5 per section. This is where courses are won or lost.

Spelling counts. `occured` instead of `occurred` is wrong. British and American spellings are both accepted (`colour` / `color`). Plurals matter — `child` when the answer is `children` is wrong.

## Study Plan

### If you have 8+ weeks

**Weeks 1–2 — Foundations.** Read all 14 [[guides|question-type guides]]. Do one passage per day, untimed, focusing on accuracy. Ignore the clock entirely.

**Weeks 3–4 — Drill by type.** Use [[drill|Drill Mode]] to do 20 questions of one type in a sitting. Matching Headings first — it's the hardest and drags scores down across the whole test. Then True/False/Not Given.

**Weeks 5–6 — Passage timing.** Full passages, 20 minutes each. Not full tests yet. Build endurance at the passage level first.

**Weeks 7–8 — Full tests.** Three full timed tests per week minimum. Review every wrong answer the same day. Track your errors by question type — your weaknesses will cluster.

### If you have 2–4 weeks

Skip untimed practice. Go straight to Drill Mode for your weakest three question types (most students: Matching Headings, True/False/Not Given, Summary Completion). One full test every 48 hours. Review matters more than volume at this stage.

### If you have under 2 weeks

Four full tests. Deep review after each. Accept that you're optimizing what you already have, not building new skill.

## The Three Habits That Move Scores

**1. Read the questions before the passage.** Always. Knowing what you're hunting for turns a 900-word passage into a targeted search. The only exception is Matching Headings, where you skim the passage first.

**2. Never re-read a paragraph without a reason.** If you didn't find the answer the first time, the problem is usually your question, not the paragraph. Re-read the question, then scan again with a sharper target.

**3. Move on after 90 seconds.** Mark it, guess, keep going. A blank answer and a wrong answer cost the same — zero. An unanswered hard question can cost you three easy ones at the end.
