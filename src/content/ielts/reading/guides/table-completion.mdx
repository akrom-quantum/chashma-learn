# Table Completion

A table with some cells filled in and some blank. Fill the empty cells using exact words from the passage, within a stated word limit.

Tables are common in passages about comparative topics — different species, competing technologies, historical periods, regional variations. The filled cells act as landmarks, helping you locate the right passage section for each blank.

---

## What's Being Tested

- Matching tabular structure to the passage's information.
- Fast lateral comparison (read across rows) or vertical comparison (read down columns).
- Exact-word transcription.

Gaps are typically in **passage order within each row or column** — but if the table organizes information by category rather than chronologically, the passage may cover the categories in a different order.

---

## Strategy

**1. Read the table headers first.**
Rows and columns tell you what dimensions of comparison the table tracks. Age? Location? Cost? Result?

**2. Look at the filled cells. Pick a specific one as a landmark.**
If a row has "Species: Monarch Butterfly" and "Habitat: ___" is blank, scan the passage for "Monarch Butterfly" — it's probably named right next to its habitat.

**3. For each blank, identify what the cell needs.**
A name? A date? A measurement? An adjective?

**4. Find the exact word(s) in the passage.**

**5. Check word limit, write the answer.**

---

## Rows vs Columns

Tables can be organized two ways:

**Row-based tables** (horizontal): each row is a complete entity (a person, species, technology), with columns showing attributes.

| Discovery | Year | Scientist | Country |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penicillin | ___ | Fleming | UK |
| X-rays | 1895 | ___ | Germany |

**Column-based tables** (vertical): each column is an entity, with rows showing attributes.

| Attribute | Species A | Species B | Species C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Habitat | ___ | wetlands | savannah |
| Diet | insects | ___ | fruit |

Passages with row-based tables often discuss each entity in turn. Passages with column-based tables often discuss each **attribute** across all entities. Scanning strategy should match.

## Worked Example

**Passage extract:**
*"Three rice-growing regions in Asia illustrate the diversity of cultivation methods. In the Mekong Delta of Vietnam, farmers practice wet-paddy cultivation, flooding their fields for most of the growing season. Yields reach 6 tonnes per hectare annually, but the method depends on reliable irrigation. In contrast, the terraced fields of northern Luzon in the Philippines rely on rainfall for much of their water supply; yields are lower at 3 tonnes per hectare, but the system sustains two harvests each year. The hill slopes of Yunnan, China, use dry-land cultivation entirely, producing a hardier variety of upland rice at modest yields of 2.5 tonnes per hectare. Yunnan farmers have maintained this approach for over two millennia."*

**Instructions: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER.**

**Table:**

| Region | Method | Water source | Annual yield (t/ha) | Harvests per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mekong Delta | wet-paddy | **(1) ___** | 6 | 1 (implied) |
| Northern Luzon | terraced | **(2) ___** | **(3) ___** | 2 |
| Yunnan | **(4) ___** | rainfall/dry | **(5) ___** | 1 (implied) |

**Working through it:**

1. *Mekong Delta water source* → passage says "depends on reliable irrigation." Answer: **irrigation**.
2. *Northern Luzon water source* → "rely on rainfall." Answer: **rainfall**.
3. *Northern Luzon yield* → "3 tonnes per hectare." Answer: **3**.
4. *Yunnan method* → "dry-land cultivation." Answer: **dry-land** (hyphenated = 1 word).
5. *Yunnan yield* → "2.5 tonnes per hectare." Answer: **2.5**.

Note how the table compresses and reorganizes the passage. The passage doesn't present itself in table form — you must map each passage claim to its cell.

---

## Time Budget

30–45 seconds per cell. Tables are fast because the structure tells you exactly what you need.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Filling rows in order instead of by difficulty.** Do easy cells first (clear landmarks nearby) to build momentum and narrow the passage.
- **Misaligning row and column.** Pay attention to which row *and* which column the blank is in.
- **Writing passage words that are in the wrong form.** If the header says "Yield (tonnes per hectare)" and you write "6 tonnes per hectare," you've duplicated the unit. Write just "6."
- **Ignoring units already provided.** If the column header is "(tonnes)," don't write "tonnes" again.

> [!tip] Use filled cells as anchors
> If a row has three filled cells and two blanks, the filled cells are your search terms. Scanning the passage for the combination of filled-cell words gets you to the right section almost instantly.
