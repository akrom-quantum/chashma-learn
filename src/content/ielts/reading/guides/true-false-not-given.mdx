# True / False / Not Given

You're given a list of factual statements. For each one, decide:

- **True** — the statement agrees with the information in the passage.
- **False** — the statement contradicts the information in the passage.
- **Not Given** — the information is neither confirmed nor contradicted.

This question type destroys more scores than any other. The "Not Given" option is what does it. Students want every statement to be True or False because Not Given feels like giving up. It isn't. It's a real answer, and it appears as often as the other two.

---

## What's Being Tested

- Whether you can distinguish what a passage **actually says** from what a passage **implies** or what you **assume**.
- Your ability to resist filling gaps with outside knowledge.
- Careful reading of qualifiers (*some*, *most*, *often*, *always*).

Statements appear in the **same order as the information in the passage**.

---

## The Three Definitions (memorize exactly)

> [!abstract] True
> The passage contains information that **matches the statement**. Paraphrased, synonymed, or rearranged — but the meaning is the same.

> [!abstract] False
> The passage contains information that **contradicts the statement**. The passage says the opposite, or makes a claim that logically excludes the statement.

> [!abstract] Not Given
> The passage **does not address the statement at all**, or addresses a related topic without confirming or denying this specific claim.

The trick: True and False both require the passage to **say something**. Not Given means the passage **didn't say**. If you can't point to a sentence that proves the statement right or wrong, it's Not Given.

---

## Strategy

**1. Read the statement. Underline the key claim.**
Not the subject — the **claim**. "Scientists in the 1960s were the first to discover X" → the claim is "first to discover in the 1960s," not just "scientists."

**2. Scan the passage for the subject of the statement.**
Find where the passage talks about this topic.

**3. Read that section carefully. Ask: does it confirm the claim, contradict the claim, or neither?**
Confirm → True. Contradict → False. Neither → Not Given.

**4. If you can't find the subject in the passage at all after a careful scan, the answer is Not Given.**

---

## The False vs Not Given Distinction

This is the hardest judgment in IELTS Reading. A rule that works every time:

> [!tip] The contradiction test
> For an answer to be **False**, the passage must state something that **cannot be true at the same time as the statement**. If the passage is merely silent on part of the claim, it's **Not Given**.

**Example of False:**
- Statement: *"The research was conducted in France."*
- Passage: *"Dr. Chen carried out all the fieldwork in northern Japan."*
- The passage directly excludes France. **False.**

**Example of Not Given:**
- Statement: *"The research was funded by the government."*
- Passage: *"Dr. Chen carried out all the fieldwork in northern Japan."*
- Funding isn't mentioned. We can't prove or disprove the claim. **Not Given.**

---

## The True vs Not Given Distinction

> [!tip] The "does it actually say that?" test
> For an answer to be **True**, the passage must support **every part** of the statement — including any adjectives, qualifiers, and numbers. If any part of the claim isn't confirmed, it's **Not Given**.

**Example of True:**
- Statement: *"Most early computers were built using vacuum tubes."*
- Passage: *"The majority of computers built before 1955 relied on vacuum tube technology."*
- "Most" matches "majority." "Before 1955" matches "early." "Built using" matches "relied on." **True.**

**Example of Not Given:**
- Statement: *"Early computers were built using vacuum tubes by a single engineer."*
- Same passage. The vacuum tubes part is supported. But "by a single engineer" isn't mentioned. **Not Given.**

## Worked Examples

**Passage extract:**
*"The Megalodon, an extinct shark that lived between 23 and 3.6 million years ago, is believed to have grown up to 18 metres in length. Fossil evidence consists almost entirely of teeth, as shark skeletons — made of cartilage rather than bone — rarely survive fossilisation. Some researchers have argued that the Megalodon's extinction coincided with the rise of the great white shark, though this theory remains contested."*

**1. Megalodon teeth were the largest of any shark species.**
- Passage mentions Megalodon teeth existed. Says nothing about their size relative to other sharks. **Not Given.**

**2. Shark skeletons fossilise less reliably than bony-fish skeletons.**
- Passage says shark skeletons "rarely survive fossilisation" because they're cartilage not bone. This directly implies bone fossilises more reliably. **True.**

**3. Scientists agree that the great white shark caused Megalodon's extinction.**
- Passage says "some researchers argued" this and that the theory "remains contested." That's the opposite of "scientists agree." **False.**

**4. The Megalodon reached 18 metres in length.**
- Passage says "believed to have grown **up to** 18 metres." "Up to" means that's the maximum, not the typical size. The statement drops the qualifier. **False** — no, wait. Re-read: "up to 18 metres" does confirm that 18m was reached. The statement doesn't say "typical" or "average." **True.**

> [!warning] The last example is the trap
> Many students answer False because "up to" felt like a hedge. But the statement isn't "every Megalodon was 18m" — just that they reached that length, which is exactly what the passage confirms. **Read the statement literally.**

## Time Budget

40–60 seconds per statement. These are fast when you trust your process. They get slow when you start second-guessing — especially on Not Given. The moment you catch yourself reading the passage a third time, pick Not Given and move on.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Using outside knowledge.** You might *know* the Megalodon was huge. Doesn't matter. Only the passage counts.
- **Assuming Not Given is rare.** In a typical 5-statement set, expect roughly 1–2 of each answer type.
- **Treating a missing detail as False.** Silence is Not Given, not False.
- **Ignoring qualifiers.** *Some*, *most*, *all*, *always*, *only* — these tiny words flip answers.
- **Writing T/F/NG instead of True/False/Not Given.** Some answer sheets accept abbreviations; many don't. **Write the full word.** Every time.

> [!danger] YNG vs TNG — don't confuse them
> If the passage contains the writer's **opinions**, the question type switches to [[yes-no-not-given|Yes / No / Not Given]]. Check the instructions before you start writing. If you write T/F/NG on a YNG question, every answer is marked wrong.
