# Diagram Label Completion

A picture or technical drawing with parts indicated by arrows or lines. Some labels are filled; others are blank. Fill the blanks using exact words from the passage, within a stated word limit.

Diagrams show physical objects — machines, biological structures, architectural features, geological formations. The passage describes the parts of the object, and you match description to visual.

---

## What's Being Tested

- Translating verbal description into spatial understanding.
- Identifying which part of the passage corresponds to which part of the diagram.
- Exact-word transcription.

Gaps are **not strictly in passage order** — a diagram label's answer depends on spatial location, not position in the text. A diagram of a windmill might describe the blades last in the passage but label them first in the diagram.

---

## Strategy

**1. Study the diagram first.**
Identify what it is. Note the filled labels — they tell you what the object is and orient you.

**2. Read the passage section that describes the object.**
Usually one paragraph or less. Read it all before attempting labels — you need the spatial picture.

**3. Match each label to the passage.**
Pay attention to directional and positional language: *above, below, beneath, adjacent to, at the base, at the top, between X and Y, running parallel to*. These tell you which part is which.

**4. Extract the exact name of each part from the passage.**

**5. Check word limit. Write the answer.**

---

## Spatial Language to Watch For

Passages describing diagrams lean on positional phrases:

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *at the top / apex* | uppermost part |
| *at the base / foot* | bottom |
| *beneath / below* | underneath |
| *on either side of X* | flanking X |
| *running from X to Y* | a connecting part between two labeled elements |
| *encased within / surrounded by* | inner part; another part wraps around it |
| *adjacent to / next to* | directly beside |
| *protrudes from / extends from* | sticks out from a base part |

Reading carefully here is the whole skill. A small misreading of "above" vs "beneath" costs you the mark.

## Worked Example

**Passage extract:**
*"A traditional water wheel consists of several key components. The wheel itself rotates on a horizontal axle, supported by a wooden frame. Around the wheel's outer rim are attached buckets or paddles that catch the flowing water. The water is directed onto the wheel by a sluice — a controlled channel that narrows the flow and concentrates its force. Above the wheel, a timber trough carries water from the millstream to the sluice. The axle extends inward and connects to a large wooden gear called the pit wheel. This gear turns a smaller vertical gear, the wallower, which transfers motion upward to the millstones where grain is ground."*

**Instructions: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage.**

**Diagram: a side-view of a water wheel** with the following blanks:
- (1) — the long timber carrying water toward the wheel (at the top of the diagram)
- (2) — the controlled channel directing water onto the wheel
- (3) — objects attached around the wheel's rim catching water
- (4) — the horizontal rod the wheel rotates on
- (5) — the first gear inside the building, attached to the axle
- (6) — the vertical gear above that transfers motion upward

**Working through it:**

1. *Long timber carrying water from millstream* → passage: "timber trough carries water from the millstream." Answer: **trough** or **timber trough**. Check passage: "a timber trough." 2 words fits. Prefer **trough** (1 word — safer, cleaner match).
2. *Controlled channel onto wheel* → passage: "sluice — a controlled channel." Answer: **sluice**.
3. *Attached around the rim* → passage: "buckets or paddles that catch the flowing water." Answer: **buckets** or **paddles**. Either works if the instructions accept alternatives — typically pick **buckets** (listed first). If unsure: **paddles**. Check mark-scheme conventions in your practice material.
4. *Horizontal rod* → passage: "horizontal axle." Answer: **axle** (or *horizontal axle*).
5. *First internal gear, on axle* → passage: "wooden gear called the pit wheel." Answer: **pit wheel**.
6. *Vertical gear above pit wheel* → passage: "smaller vertical gear, the wallower." Answer: **wallower**.

---

## Time Budget

30–45 seconds per label. Once the passage section is located, labels fill quickly because each is a discrete named object.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Scanning the passage before studying the diagram.** You need the visual first, or passage words won't map to anything.
- **Ignoring positional language in the passage.** "Above" and "below" flip the meaning of adjacent answers.
- **Writing a description instead of the part's name.** The label wants the name ("wallower"), not an explanation ("the smaller vertical gear").
- **Missing that two labels are both valid options** (like "buckets or paddles"). If the passage offers both, write one — usually the first named, unless grammar or usual convention favours the other.

> [!tip] Cross-check with labelled parts
> The already-filled labels anchor you. If "millstones" is a filled label at the top-right of the diagram and the passage mentions millstones last in its description, the flow of the passage maps to the flow of the diagram. Use filled labels to triangulate unknown ones.
