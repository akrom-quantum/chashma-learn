# Sentence Completion

You're given incomplete sentences. Complete each one using words **taken directly from the passage** — no more than the stated limit (usually one, two, or three words).

**Critical rule:** the missing words must appear in the passage exactly as you write them. No synonyms, no paraphrasing, no changing form. Copy, don't compose.

---

## What's Being Tested

- Locating specific detail fast.
- Reading the passage's exact wording.
- Understanding grammar well enough to pick the right form of the answer.
- Following word-limit instructions precisely.

Sentences are in **passage order**.

---

## The Word Limit Rule

Instructions say one of:
- **"NO MORE THAN ONE WORD"** — write 1 word only.
- **"NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS"** — write 1 or 2 words.
- **"NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS"** — write 1, 2, or 3 words.
- **"NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER"** — up to 2 words, possibly plus a digit.

**Hyphenated words count as one word.** *Well-known* = 1 word. *Twenty-first century* = 2 words.

**Numbers count as one word regardless of digit count.** *1879* = 1 word. *2.5 million* = 2 words.

> [!danger] One word over the limit = zero marks
> If the limit is 2 words and the passage says "remarkably effective treatment" and the answer is really just "effective treatment," writing all three words is wrong. Even if "remarkably" is technically in the passage. Follow the limit strictly.

---

## Strategy

**1. Read the incomplete sentence. Identify the grammatical slot.**
Is the missing word a noun? Adjective? Verb? Number? The grammar tells you what to look for.

*"The study was conducted over a period of ___ years."* → Needs a number.
*"The main ___ of the experiment was to measure reaction time."* → Needs a noun (purpose, aim, goal, focus…).
*"Samples were ___ at regular intervals."* → Needs a past participle verb (collected, taken, measured…).

**2. Underline the keywords in the sentence.**
Nouns that will appear (or be paraphrased) in the passage.

**3. Scan the passage for those keywords.**
Find the matching section.

**4. Read carefully to identify the exact words that fit the gap.**

**5. Copy them exactly — including spelling, capitalization, and plural form.**

**6. Check against the word limit.**

---

## The Paraphrase Mismatch

The sentence in the question paraphrases the passage. But your **answer** must come from the passage in its exact form.

**Passage:** *"Early thermometers used mercury, which expands predictably when heated."*

**Question:** *"Early thermometers relied on ___, which expands in a predictable way when temperature rises."*

**Answer:** **mercury** (not "a liquid metal" — even though that's accurate).

The sentence around the gap is paraphrased; the missing word isn't. This is the whole skill of this question type.

## Worked Example

**Passage extract:**
*"The manufacture of paper in Europe relied for centuries on linen rags as the primary raw material. By the early 19th century, increased demand from newspapers outstripped rag supply, leading to a critical shortage. Friedrich Gottlob Keller, a German weaver, developed a mechanical process to produce paper from wood pulp in 1843. His invention used a grindstone to separate fibres, producing a coarser but vastly cheaper paper. Commercial production expanded rapidly, and by 1880 wood-based paper had largely displaced rag-based paper across Europe."*

**Instructions: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage.**

**1. Before the 19th century, European paper was produced primarily from ___.**
- Passage: "relied for centuries on linen rags" → the material is **linen rags**.
- 2 words. Fits the limit. **Answer: linen rags.**

**2. A shortage of raw material was caused by increased demand from ___.**
- Passage: "increased demand from newspapers." Answer: **newspapers**.
- 1 word. **Answer: newspapers.**

**3. Keller's new process relied on a ___ to separate wood fibres.**
- Passage: "used a grindstone to separate fibres." Answer: **grindstone**.
- 1 word. **Answer: grindstone.**

**4. By 1880, paper made from wood had largely replaced paper made from ___.**
- Passage: "wood-based paper had largely displaced rag-based paper." The question uses "paper made from wood" → the contrast is "paper made from rags." From the passage we need the form that matches — "rag-based" or "rags"?
- "Rag-based" is hyphenated (counts as 1 word) and directly paraphrases "paper made from rags." **Answer: rag-based.**
- Alternative: if the passage had said "rags" alone as a noun, that would work too. Use whichever form appears in the passage and fits grammatically.

---

## Time Budget

40–60 seconds per sentence. This is one of the faster question types — if you're slow here, you're not using passage order to narrow your scan.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Paraphrasing your answer.** Write exactly what the passage says, not what you think it means.
- **Using your own word.** Even if your word is a perfect synonym, it's wrong.
- **Exceeding the word limit.** Check every answer against the limit before transferring.
- **Wrong grammatical form.** If the sentence needs a singular noun and the passage has a plural, adjust only if the passage has both forms. Otherwise the passage wording wins.
- **Spelling errors.** Copy letter-by-letter. A wrong spelling is a wrong answer.
- **Writing the whole sentence instead of just the missing part.**

> [!tip] When two word-counts could work
> If both "the Roman Empire" (3 words) and "Romans" (1 word) appear in the passage and either could complete the sentence, pick the one that fits the grammatical slot most naturally. Usually the shorter version is safer if both work.
