# Note Completion

You're given a set of notes in abbreviated form — often bullet points under headings — with gaps. Fill them using exact words from the passage, within a stated word limit.

The notes are a compressed rewrite of part of the passage, organized by category (often by stages, groups, or features). Think of them as someone's skeletal revision notes that you're completing.

---

## What's Being Tested

- Fast retrieval of specific detail from a well-organised passage.
- Ability to follow the passage's internal structure.
- Exact-word transcription.

Gaps are in **passage order** within the noted section. Unlike Summary Completion, notes tend to be bullet-based with clear categorical headings, making the structure obvious.

---

## Strategy

**1. Read the note structure first.**
The headings tell you what sections of the passage to find. If the notes are organized under *"Early life," "Scientific career," "Legacy,"* you know to find the biographical passage and work through it in order.

**2. Scan the passage for the first section's section.**
The first heading's content usually appears in one block of the passage — find that block.

**3. For each gap, identify the grammatical and meaning requirement.**

**4. Pull the exact word(s) from the passage. Check word limit.**

**5. Move to the next heading. Locate its section. Repeat.**

---

## What Makes Note Completion Different from Sentence Completion

- **Notes are telegraphic** — they drop articles (*the, a*) and verbs. Your answer follows the note's style but must still be a word from the passage.
- **Grammar slots are looser** — you might need a noun where a sentence would need a full clause.
- **Structure is explicit** — headings guide you to the right paragraph faster than sentence-completion stems do.

---

## Worked Example

**Passage extract:**
*"The construction of the Channel Tunnel, linking England and France beneath the English Sea, took six years and involved over 13,000 engineers, technicians, and support workers. Work began in 1988, with tunnelling teams boring simultaneously from both sides. The two bores met beneath the sea in 1990, missing the target alignment by just 358 millimetres — remarkable precision across 37 kilometres. The tunnel opened to rail traffic in 1994 at a total cost of £4.65 billion, substantially over budget. Since opening, over 430 million passengers have travelled through it, though concerns about ageing infrastructure have grown in recent years, prompting a major maintenance programme in 2018."*

**Instructions: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER.**

**Notes:**

### Channel Tunnel — Key Facts

- Construction duration: **(1) ___** years
- Number of workers involved: over **(2) ___**
- Start year: **(3) ___**
- Two bores met: in **(4) ___**
- Alignment error: only **(5) ___** millimetres
- Total cost: £**(6) ___**
- Total passengers (so far): over **(7) ___**
- Major maintenance began: in **(8) ___**

**Working through it:**

1. *"took six years"* → **six** (or **6** — either is usually accepted, but match the passage: "six").
2. *"over 13,000 engineers, technicians…"* → **13,000**.
3. *"Work began in 1988"* → **1988**.
4. *"met beneath the sea in 1990"* → **1990**.
5. *"missing the target alignment by just 358 millimetres"* → **358**.
6. *"£4.65 billion"* → **4.65 billion** (2 words).
7. *"over 430 million passengers"* → **430 million** (2 words).
8. *"major maintenance programme in 2018"* → **2018**.

All within the 2-word/number limit.

---

## Time Budget

30–45 seconds per gap. This is typically the fastest question type — the note structure reduces scanning effort dramatically.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Writing more than the word limit.** The telegraphic style of notes tempts students to include extra context. Stick to the limit.
- **Misreading the heading.** If a gap is under "Legacy" and you're scanning the early-career section of the passage, you won't find it.
- **Writing numbers in words when the passage uses digits (or vice versa).** Copy the passage's form.
- **Making up connecting words.** If the note reads *"Start year: ___,"* the answer is "1988" — not "in 1988" or "year 1988."

> [!tip] Notes as a roadmap
> Because note completion mirrors the passage's structure, doing this question type first can give you a free outline of the passage — useful for other questions about the same text.
