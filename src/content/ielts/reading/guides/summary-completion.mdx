# Summary Completion

You're given a paragraph-length summary of part of the passage with several gaps. Fill the gaps. Two variants exist:

**Variant A — fill from the passage.**
Same rules as [[sentence-completion|Sentence Completion]]: use exact words from the passage, within a stated word limit.

**Variant B — fill from a word bank.**
You're given a list of ~10 words; pick the right one for each gap. There are more words than gaps, so some are distractors. Answers do **not** need to come from the passage in this variant — they're in the word bank.

The instructions tell you which variant. Check before you start.

---

## What's Being Tested

- Understanding of a **section** of the passage, not the whole thing.
- Fitting the right word into both the grammatical slot and the meaning.
- For Variant B: recognition of synonyms (word bank uses synonyms of passage wording).

Gaps are in **passage order** within the summarized section. The summary usually covers only part of the passage — identify which part before you start.

---

## Strategy (Variant A — from passage)

Same as [[sentence-completion|Sentence Completion]], with one added step: **identify which part of the passage the summary covers first.**

**1. Read the summary title or first sentence. Match it to the passage.**
Is this summary about Darwin's early life, or his voyage, or his theory? The summary might cover paragraphs 2–4 only. Don't scan the whole passage.

**2. Read the summary once, gap by gap, noticing what grammar each gap needs.**

**3. Work through gap by gap. Scan the relevant passage section. Find the exact word.**

**4. Copy exactly; check word limit.**

---

## Strategy (Variant B — from word bank)

**1. Read the full summary once to grasp the meaning.**
You need the whole picture before filling gaps, because word-bank choices often depend on context.

**2. Read the word bank. Identify each word's part of speech.**
The bank might have 5 nouns, 3 adjectives, 2 verbs. Noting this makes each gap easier.

**3. For each gap, identify: what part of speech is needed? What meaning does the sentence demand?**

**4. Scan the passage for the matching information.**
The passage will contain a synonym of the word bank answer. Your job is to find the passage meaning, then pick the bank word that matches.

**5. Cross off bank words as you use them.**
Unless instructions say words can be reused, each word is used once.

---

## The Variant B Synonym Trap

The word bank contains near-synonyms of each other — that's the trap. *Rapid* and *sudden*. *Significant* and *remarkable*. The correct one must match the **passage's exact meaning**, not just sound right.

> [!warning] Example of the trap
> Passage: *"The population grew slowly but consistently over a century."*
> Word bank: **steady, rapid, explosive, unpredictable, declining.**
> Gap: *"Population growth was ___."*
> Both "steady" and "slow" could describe "slowly but consistently." But "rapid" and "explosive" contradict the passage. The best fit is **steady** — it captures the consistency emphasized by the passage.

## Worked Example (Variant B — with word bank)

**Passage extract:**
*"Mount Fuji, Japan's highest peak at 3,776 metres, is an active stratovolcano, though its last eruption occurred in 1707. The mountain has been revered in Japanese culture for centuries, regarded as sacred by both Shinto and Buddhist traditions. Its near-perfect symmetrical cone has inspired countless artworks, most famously the series of woodblock prints by Hokusai. Despite being a tourist destination attracting hundreds of thousands of climbers every summer, the mountain remains closed to climbing during the off-season due to severe weather conditions."*

**Word bank (choose from A–J):**
- A. dormant
- B. worshipped
- C. inactive
- D. avoided
- E. striking
- F. forbidden
- G. destroyed
- H. distinctive
- I. harsh
- J. regular

**Summary with gaps:**
Mount Fuji, though considered **(1) ___** by some scientists, has not erupted since 1707. For centuries, it has been **(2) ___** by followers of both Shinto and Buddhism. The mountain's **(3) ___** shape has inspired famous artworks, including prints by Hokusai. It is **(4) ___** during winter months because of **(5) ___** weather.

**Working through it:**

1. *"considered ___ by some scientists"* — passage says "active stratovolcano" but "last eruption in 1707." Something dormant or inactive might fit. Both A (dormant) and C (inactive) are candidates. Passage says "active," but 1707 is 300+ years ago — scientists might debate. Volcanic terminology: "dormant" is more precise. **A — dormant.**

2. *"has been ___ by followers"* — passage: "revered… sacred by Shinto and Buddhist traditions." Match: **B — worshipped.**

3. *"The mountain's ___ shape"* — passage: "near-perfect symmetrical cone." The cone is visually notable. **E (striking)** and **H (distinctive)** are candidates. "Striking" implies dramatic impact; "distinctive" implies uniquely recognizable. Hokusai's prints focus on Fuji's unmistakable cone → "distinctive" captures recognition. But "striking" captures visual impact. Either could work; "distinctive" matches "near-perfect symmetrical" more closely. **H — distinctive.**

4. *"It is ___ during winter months"* — passage: "closed to climbing during the off-season." Match: **F — forbidden.** (*Avoided* is weaker — the mountain isn't merely avoided, it's officially closed.)

5. *"because of ___ weather"* — passage: "severe weather conditions." Match: **I — harsh.**

**Answers:** 1-A, 2-B, 3-H, 4-F, 5-I.
**Unused:** C, D, E, G, J.

---

## Time Budget

**Variant A:** 60–90 seconds per gap.
**Variant B:** 45–60 seconds per gap (word bank narrows choices).

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Not identifying the summary's scope.** If you scan the whole passage for every gap, you waste time.
- **Paraphrasing in Variant A.** Exact passage words only.
- **Using a bank word just because it fits grammatically.** Meaning has to match the passage.
- **Using a bank word twice when reuse isn't allowed.** Check instructions.
- **Ignoring word forms in Variant A.** If the passage has "preserve" and the gap needs an adjective, that won't work — find the adjective form or a different word from the passage.

> [!tip] The re-read test
> After filling all gaps, read the completed summary end to end. It should sound like natural, grammatical English that matches the passage. Any sentence that reads awkwardly usually has a wrong answer.
