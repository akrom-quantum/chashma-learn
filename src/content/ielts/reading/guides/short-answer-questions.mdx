# Short-answer Questions

Direct questions — *Who, What, When, Where, Why, How* — answered with words from the passage, within a stated word limit.

This is the most straightforward IELTS Reading question type. If you follow the question literally and obey the word limit, marks are easy. Students lose marks by overwriting or by answering a slightly different question than what's asked.

---

## What's Being Tested

- Locating specific factual detail.
- Parsing *wh-* questions accurately.
- Respecting the word limit.

Questions are in **passage order**.

---

## Strategy

**1. Read the question carefully.**
Identify the question word (Who/What/When/Where/Why/How) — it tells you what kind of answer you need.

- **Who** → a person, role, or group.
- **What** → a thing, concept, action, or number.
- **When** → a time, date, period.
- **Where** → a place or location.
- **Why** → a reason — look for *because, due to, in order to, owing to* in the passage.
- **How** → a method, manner, or degree.

**2. Identify the key noun in the question.**
*"Why did Roman soldiers value Egyptian wheat?"* → key nouns are *Roman soldiers* and *Egyptian wheat*. Scan for either.

**3. Find the passage sentence. Extract the answer.**

**4. Match the word limit exactly. Do not add articles, helper verbs, or explanatory words.**

---

## The Minimal-Answer Rule

Short-answer questions punish verbosity. If the limit is two words and the answer is one word, write one word. Don't pad.

**Question:** *"What preserved the Pompeii artifacts?"*
**Passage:** *"The ash from the eruption buried the city, preserving wooden structures, frescoes, and even loaves of bread in a state that archaeologists would find two millennia later."*
**Correct answer:** **ash** (1 word).
**Wrong answer:** **volcanic ash** (not in passage — "volcanic" isn't there).
**Wrong answer:** **the ash** (1 word within limit but stylistically includes an article — usually accepted, sometimes not. Safer: just "ash").

## Worked Example

**Passage extract:**
*"The Silk Road was not, despite its name, a single road. It was a network of overland routes stretching roughly 6,400 kilometres from Xi'an in China to Constantinople (modern Istanbul). The name was coined in 1877 by German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen, more than a thousand years after the network's peak activity. Goods carried along these routes included not only silk but also spices, precious stones, paper, gunpowder, and ideas — the last of which historians often consider the most consequential. Buddhism reached China along the Silk Road, as did, centuries later, the Black Death, which killed an estimated one-third of Europe's population after arriving via the same trade routes."*

**Instructions: NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS from the passage.**

**1. What did the Silk Road's name suggest it was, incorrectly?**
- Passage: "was not, despite its name, a single road." Answer: **a single road** (3 words — at limit).

**2. How long was the Silk Road network?**
- Passage: "stretching roughly 6,400 kilometres." Answer: **6,400 kilometres** (2 words).

**3. Who coined the name "Silk Road"?**
- Passage: "by German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen." Answer: **Ferdinand von Richthofen** (3 words).

**4. In what year was the name created?**
- Passage: "in 1877." Answer: **1877**.

**5. According to historians, what was the most important thing carried along the Silk Road?**
- Passage: "ideas — the last of which historians often consider the most consequential." Answer: **ideas**.

**6. What disease reached Europe via the Silk Road?**
- Passage: "the Black Death, which killed an estimated one-third of Europe's population." Answer: **the Black Death** (3 words) or **Black Death** (2 words). Both accepted; the shorter form is safer.

---

## Time Budget

30–45 seconds per question. Short-answer is designed to be quick. If you're at 90 seconds on one of these, you're over-thinking — scan more aggressively.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Exceeding the word limit.** The single most frequent error. Count every word before committing.
- **Writing full sentences instead of minimal answers.** *"The Silk Road was discovered by…"* is not an answer. The answer is the name.
- **Answering the wrong *wh-* word.** If the question is "Why," you need a reason. An event, date, or name is wrong — even if it's in the passage near the topic.
- **Adding words the passage doesn't have.** If the passage says "ash" and you write "volcanic ash," the extra word isn't from the passage.
- **Overuse of articles.** "The" and "a" are typically tolerated but unnecessary. Omit to save word count for genuine content.

> [!tip] When the answer seems longer than the limit allows
> Re-read the question. You've probably answered a different question than the one asked. The correct answer for a 3-word limit is always extractable in 3 words from the passage — if yours isn't, you're targeting the wrong detail.
